Professional Outreach

Learning from Community

When I began my journey as an information professional, I had no idea where this journey would take me. Ten years ago, I was making the decision to leave academia for a contract research position. I guess it could still be considered academia, but I stopped pursuing my doctorate. I had spent my graduate school studies struggling to be able to write papers. I had come to the realization that I wanted to spend more time making history available to others. I loved the research aspect of historical studies. This naturally led me back to working with libraries and over the years, as I worked up the courage to go back to school for another degree, I discovered something that was almost more valuable than a course on cataloging or how to do research as a librarian. I discovered the legal information professional community!

As a non legal professional within the legal sector, I had to learn quickly while also practising all those skills taught through information studies degree programs that I had not yet pursued. I spent countless hours, pouring over articles and blogs, learning everything I could about metadata and collection development and I found a community with which to share my findings and discuss. First, I joined the Federation des milieux documentaires (then ASTED) where I could talk metadata to my heart's content. I discussed the practical applications of RDA in French, how could we change batch change authority records and lessons learned about the new Library of Congress Subject and Forms.

Then, I decided to join the Association des bibliotheques de droit de Montreal and the Canadian Association of Law Libraries CALL/ACBD. These two associations offered me a wealth of support and community in a very small community. We say that the library world is small and the law library world is even smaller! I got to learn from some amazing mentors who volunteered their time to listen and to answer my myriad questions. I have made connections with legal information professionals across the country and have this network available to help whenever I have a question. For example, during the COVID-19 pandemic, discussion groups were held about how libraries were trying to continue to serve their clients as libraries were closed in most offices and courthouses aside from one or two people, yet the justice system was not stopped. These communities allowed us to exchange our best practices.

Finally, the opportunities for targeted professional development offered here was indispensable. While there is a course on legal information in the MIsT program at McGill, there is so much that is not taught for lack of time. The webinars and programs offered, including the Law Librarian's Institute were invaluable in helping me hone my legal research and retrieval skills.

These same communities have also embraced my interest in data and user experience and I am thrilled that I now get to continue to participate in these associations as a member of their boards, but more importantly, I now get to act as a mentor to those entering the profession. There is so much to learn and explore and discuss that I find it really rewarding to see the advancements of legal information but also the openness of such a community who took me in when I was still unsure about a) legal information and b) librarianship. Ten years in, and this has been the most rewarding years of my life as I continue to gain valuable skills to understand all aspects of the legal information environment.
